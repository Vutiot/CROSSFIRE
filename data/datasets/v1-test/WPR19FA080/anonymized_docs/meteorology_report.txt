A. ACCIDENT
Location: Location-1, Region-West
Date: March 24, 2019
Time: about 1815 Pacific standard time
0215 Universal Coordinated Time (UTC) on March 25, 2019
Airplane: Cessna 172M; Registration: N-XXXX

B. METEOROLOGIST
Meteorologist-1
Senior Meteorologist
Operational Factors Division (AS-30)
National Transportation Safety Board

C. SUMMARY
On March 24, 2019, about 1815 Pacific Standard time, a Cessna 172M airplane, N-XXXX,
impacted mountainous terrain near Location-1, Region-West while on a cross-country flight
to Airport-Bravo, Location-3, Region-West. The commercial pilot and his non pilot-
rated passenger received fatal injuries, and the airplane was destroyed. The airplane was
registered to Airline-A, and was being operated as a 14 Code of Federal Regulations Part 91
personal flight. Undetermined night meteorological conditions existed at the impact location at
the time of the accident. The accident flight leg had originated from Airport-Alpha,
Location-2, Region-West.

D. DETAILS OF THE INVESTIGATION
The National Transportation Safety Board Senior Meteorologist was not on scene for this
investigation and conducted the meteorology phase of the investigation from the Washington D.C.
office, collecting data from official National Weather Service (NWS) sources including the
Weather Prediction Center (WPC) and the National Center for Environmental Information (NCEI).
All times are Pacific standard time (PST) based upon the 24-hour clock, local time is -8 hours from
UTC, and UTC=Z. Heights are in feet (ft) above mean sea level (msl) unless otherwise noted.
The accident site is based on the reported coordinates of latitude 34.0260 N and longitude
116.5879 W at an elevation of about 2,500 ft.
E. WEATHER INFORMATION

1.0 Synoptic Conditions
Large scale weather systems were documented using standard NWS charts. Reference:
NWS/FAA Advisory Circular Aviation Weather Services AC 00-45H.

1.1 Surface Analysis Chart
NWS Surface Analysis Chart for 1900 PST depicted a low-pressure system at 999-hPa over
eastern Utah with a cold front extending southwestward through Utah, Arizona, into the Baja of
Mexico. The accident site was located south of the trough and behind the cold front.

1.2 National Composite Radar Mosaic
The National Composite Radar Mosaic for 1815 PST depicted several bands of light to moderate
intensity echoes associated with rain and/or snow showers over the high terrain north of the route
of flight and a small area of echoes located about 4 miles west of the accident site.

1.3 12-hour Surface Prognostic Chart
The chart depicted the accident site in an area of general westerly winds behind the cold front.
Location-3, Region-West indicated potential marginal conditions with clouds and precipitation.
