C. SUMMARY
On March 24, 2019, about 1815 Pacific Standard time, a Cessna 172M airplane, N-XXXX,
impacted mountainous terrain near Location-1, Region-West while on a cross-country flight
to Airport-Bravo, Location-3, Region-West. The commercial pilot and his non pilot-
rated passenger received fatal injuries, and the airplane was destroyed. The airplane was
registered to Airline-A, and was being operated as a 14 Code of
Federal Regulations Part 91 personal flight. Undetermined night meteorological conditions
existed at the impact location at the time of the accident. The accident flight leg had originated
from Airport-Alpha, Location-2, Region-West.
No evidence consistent with in-flight fire or in-flight structural failure was observed.
D. RESPONSE and SITE LOCATION INFORMATION
1.0 Background and Response
The pilot was conducting a VFR flight to Airport-Bravo from the LA Basin
He was using FAA flight following services and had been assigned a discrete beacon code
The FAA controller was in voice communications with the pilot, and was receiving the
secondary radar returns from the airplane
The pilot advised the controller that he planned to follow a road and remain at an altitude of
3,500 ft (msl) for the entire flight

Voice communications and radar returns from the airplane were lost about 1815
An FAA ALNOT was issued at 1843
Riverside Sheriffs Department personnel located the wreckage in the evening of March 24
Their initial assessment was that the pilot, believed to be the sole person on board, was
fatally injured
FAA inspectors from Riverside FSDO arrived in the morning of March 25
NTSB IIC arrived the afternoon of March 25
The Textron Aviation representative arrived the morning of March 26
2.0 Location, Site Condition, and Ground Scar Information
The accident site coordinates were 34.02574 N, -116.58775 W
The accident site elevation was about 2,465 ft, above and west of the road that the pilot was
believed to be following
The wreckage was about 800 ft west of the north-south road, which was a paved 4-lane
highway (CA Rte 62) that was situated in the river/wash valley that formed a pass through a
small mountain range
The wreckage was on steep, unstable terrain about 300 ft above the road
The wreckage was observed to be precariously situated on the mountain slope, which limited
the extent of the examination of the wreckage in situ
Only a very few items (lower cowl, windscreen fragments, left and right navigation light lens
fragments, right-side cabin door, and one MLG tire) were separated from the main wreckage
o  The lower cowl and the nav light lens fragments were found about 30 ft above the
main wreckage; this is considered to be the impact point
o  The terrain at the impact point and between that and the wreckage was steeper than
the terrain where the wreckage came to rest
o  The observed ground scars (disturbed soil and rocks) were consistent with the airplane
then falling/sliding down to its final resting location
o  The relative locations of the left and right lens fragments were congruent with the
airplane being upright when it struck the mountain face
The forward section of the airplane was upright, and partially covered with soil/rocks that
had slid downhill
The fuselage was severely crushed and torn, and the wings and empennage were severely
torn and deformed
The approximate orientation of the wreckage was on a heading of approximately 230 degrees True
o  The mountain impact face and wreckage distribution were consistent with this also
being the approximate ground track at the time of impact
There was no indication of fire, either inflight or post impact
First responders reported that the airplane was "covered in fuel" when they arrived at the
wreckage (about 0050 March 25)

E. WRECKAGE INFORMATION
1.0 In-Situ Examination
The engine and propeller were nearly completely obscured by the wreckage and/or terrain
debris that slid downhill onto the wreckage
The engine appeared to be upright
The cabin was crushed, torn and deformed, and unrecognizable except by its location
The MLG carry-through structure, including the two MLG legs, was inverted and translated
up and forward from its normal location
The aft fuselage was folded over, up, and forward of its normal position; it was also rotated
about 90 degrees (top surface to airplane left) about its longitudinal axis
The horizontal stabilizer was mostly intact, but mostly separated from the aft fuselage
Both elevators remained attached to the horizontal stabilizer, and the pitch trim tab remained
attached to the right elevator
The fin remained attached to the aft fuselage, and the rudder remained attached to the fin
Both wings were upright, but crushed, torn, and swept aft about 45 degrees
Both wings had LE aft crushing continuously across their full spans
Both ailerons remained attached to the wings
Both flaps remained attached to their wings, and appeared to be fully retracted
Both fuel caps were observed installed securely in their respective filler necks
All flight control surface balance weights appeared to be present
Multiple control cables were visible, but their routing and continuity could not be determined
due to the level of damage
Only a portion of one individual, a male, was able to be seen at first; the total number of
occupants was initially unable to be determined, due to the condition of the wreckage
Some personal items and luggage was able to be accessed from the wreckage while it was on
the hillside
o  The items included the reported pilot's wallet with his pilot and medical certificates
o  A small piece of hand luggage appeared to belong to a female, and when it was
opened, it contained female clothing and accessories
The luggage indications prompted further attempts to locate additional victims in the
wreckage, and another person (likely female) was detected
2.0 Post-Hillside Recovery
The condition and location of the wreckage warranted its extraction by helicopter
The helicopter crew packaged and recovered the wreckage to a clearing about a mile from
the accident site
The two (and only) victims were recovered from the wreckage
The wreckage was partially examined and photographed and loaded onto a truck for transport
to a secure facility where it could be examined in detail
The all metal fixed pitch two blade propeller was fracture-separated from the engine at its
attach flange

The onboard clock was stopped at 0115:47 (consistent with being set to UTC time,
equivalent to 1815:47 PST)
The artificial horizon was fracture-separated from the instrument panel
o  It was date stamped January 2, 2019
No other useful instrument or control indications were able to be made at this time
