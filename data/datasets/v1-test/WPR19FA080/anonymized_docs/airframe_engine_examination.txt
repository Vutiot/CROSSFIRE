C. SUMMARY
The wreckage was removed from the mountainside and transported to Facility-1 in
Location-8, Region-West on March 26, 2019. Detailed examination of the airframe and engine was conducted
by the NTSB and parties on March 27, 2019 at Facility-1. No evidence of preimpact
mechanical malfunction, or any pre-or post-impact fire was noted during the examination.

D. AIRFRAME EXAMINATION

1.0 General
On-scene information and overall damage patterns were consistent with the airplane
impacting a very steep (approx. 70 degrees from the horizontal) terrain in an upright,
approximately wings- and pitch- level attitude.
All primary components were located at the impact site.

2.0 Fuselage
The cockpit/cabin area was severely disrupted due to crushing and tearing.
The engine was pushed aft into the cabin.
The cabin floor, starting at the firewall, was crushed aft to the main gear attach points.

3.0 Wings
The outboard 3 ft of the left wing leading edge was crushed diagonally aft.
Both fuel caps remained securely fastened in their respective filler necks.

4.0 Flight Controls
All flight controls were present and attached to their respective aerodynamic surfaces.
Control cable continuity was established for the ailerons, elevators, pitch trim, and rudder.
The pitch trim actuator was found in a position corresponding to tab 5 degrees TEU.
The flaps were fully retracted; the flap position indicator registered 40 degrees (full down).

5.0 Controls and Instruments
The tachometer registered 6967.2 hours, and the needle pointed to about 1,600 rpm.
The altimeter Kollsman window was set to 29.82.
The attitude indicator was laid over to the right (left wing down attitude).
Fuel selector set to the right tank.
ELT: ACK Technologies model E-01, serial number 033768 - armed but had not activated.
Battery due date was 12/31/24.
A flight school document found in the airplane indicated tachometer registered 6966 hrs when
the airplane was released to the pilot.
F. ENGINE EXAMINATION

1.0 General
The powerplant was a Lycoming O-320 E2D series engine; four cylinder; air cooled, direct
drive, horizontally opposed, normally aspirated (carburetor), rated at 150hp @ 2700rpm.
Serial # L-44359-27A
Most recent overhaul 9/13/18 (264.8 hours since overhaul)
Most recent annual 11/2/18
Visual examination of the engine revealed no evidence of pre-impact catastrophic mechanical malfunction or fire.

2.0 Engine Internal and Drive Train
The crankshaft and attached connecting rods were free of heat distress.
The camshaft was intact and each of the cam lobes exhibited its characteristic elliptical shape.
There was no evidence of lubrication deprivation.
No signatures or conditions observed consistent with any pre-impact catastrophic mechanical malfunction.

3.0 Fuel System
The fuel system utilizes gravity fed fuel; there is no engine driven fuel pump.
The carburetor was fracture-separated from the engine.
The foam filter element remained securely attached to the airbox bracket; no pre-impact obstruction to airflow.

4.0 Exhaust System
No oil residue observed in the exhaust system gas path.
Maintenance records indicate the muffler and heat shroud were replaced at engine overhaul.
Entry dated 10/15/2018 @ Tach 6702.7.

G. PROPELLER EXAMINATION
The metal two bladed fixed pitch propeller was fracture-separated from the crankshaft flange.
The fracture surfaces exhibited signatures consistent with overload due to the absorption of rotational energy.
The propeller blades displayed varying degrees of leading edge gouging, torsional twisting,
chordwise striations across the cambered surface, and trailing edge S bending.
The signatures were consistent with the absorption of rotational forces applied at the
crankshaft at the time of impact.
