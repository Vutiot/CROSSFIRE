The use of deadly force is permitted only as a "last resort" when "necessary to protect
against an imminent threat to life or to prevent great bodily harm to the member or another
person."46 A Department member may use deadly force in only two situations: (1) to prevent
"death or great bodily harm from an imminent threat posed to the sworn member or to another
person"; (2) to prevent "an arrest from being defeated by resistance or escape, where the person to
be arrested poses an imminent threat of death or great bodily harm to a sworn member or another
person unless arrested without delay."47 A threat is considered imminent "when it is objectively
reasonable to believe that: (1) the person's actions are immediately likely to cause death or great
bodily harm to the member or others unless action is taken; and (2) the person has the means or
instruments to cause death or great bodily harm; and (3) the person has the opportunity and ability
to cause death or great bodily harm."48

b. Standard of Proof

For each Allegation, COPA must make one of the following findings:
1. Sustained - where it is determined the allegation is supported by a preponderance of the evidence;
2. Not Sustained - where it is determined there is insufficient evidence to prove the allegations
by a preponderance of the evidence;
3. Unfounded - where it is determined by clear and convincing evidence that an allegation is false
or not factual; or
4. Exonerated - where it is determined by clear and convincing evidence that the conduct
described in the allegation occurred, but it is lawful and proper.

A preponderance of evidence can be described as evidence indicating that it is more likely than
not that a proposition is proved.49 If the evidence gathered in an investigation establishes that it is
more likely that the misconduct occurred, even if by a narrow margin, then the preponderance of
the evidence standard is met.

Clear and convincing evidence is a higher standard than a preponderance of the evidence but
lower than the "beyond-a-reasonable doubt" standard required to convict a person of a criminal
offense.50 Clear and Convincing can be defined as a "degree of proof, which, considering all the
evidence in the case, produces the firm and abiding belief that it is highly probable that the
proposition . . . is true."51

IV. ANALYSIS AND CONCLUSION

a. Allegation #1 that Officer-1 discharged a firearm at
without justification is Sustained.

COPA finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Officer-1's use of deadly force
against violated Department policy. Based on the totality of the circumstance, COPA
finds that his weapons discharge was not objectively reasonable, necessary, or proportional. Here,
there is no evidence in the record to suggest that the gunshot wound to head was
self-inflicted, or that was armed or posed an imminent threat to Officer-1. Officer-1
did not call 911 or otherwise report an incident that would have justified his use of force.
Rather, the medical examiner ruled death a homicide and Officer-1 has been
charged with her murder.52 Although Officer-1 has not been convicted of the charged offense
and is presumed innocent until proven guilty in a court of law, these administrative proceedings
carry a lower burden of proof than criminal proceedings.53 The charges pending against Officer-1
are relevant to COPA's investigation and support the finding that Officer-1 engaged in
misconduct contrary to Department policy.54 However, even absent the criminal charges against
Officer-1, COPA finds ample factual support in the record to conclude by a preponderance of
the evidence that Officer-1 discharged his firearm at without justification.

c. Allegation #3 that Officer-1 failed to secure a firearm registered to him is
Sustained.

Department policy requires sworn members "to secure ALL firearms which they own or
possess, in a manner that a reasonable person would believe will prohibit access by unapproved
individuals, theft, or loss."57 In addition, Department members are required to secure all firearms
in their possession or under their control in a manner that will prevent them from being accessed
or used by children.58 To protect children from accessing firearms, Department members are
required to secure their firearms by either a device or mechanism that would render the firearm
temporarily inoperable, place it in a secure locked box or container, or place it somewhere that a
reasonable person would believe it to be secure from a minor under the age of 14 years.59
Department policy also prohibits officers from lending their firearms to others.60

Here, the evidence shows that Officer-1 failed to secure two of his firearms. First,
Officer-1 admitted that he had left a firearm on the top shelf of bedroom closet,
which was never found. By his own admission, Officer-1 had lent a firearm to in
violation of Department policy. Second, Officer-1 kept a firearm at home for her
protection.61 Evidence technicians subsequently recovered a firearm62 in a bedroom closet inside
residence along with one live round and one magazine.63 The firearm was found in a
holster with ammunition located nearby in a paper gift bag. This evidence shows that Officer-1
had kept not one, but two firearms in a manner that would allow unapproved individuals to have
access to them. Moreover, there is evidence that there were children under the age of 14 years
living at and residences who could have gained access to these
unsecured weapons. The preponderance of the evidence indicates that Officer-1 failed to
properly secure his firearms. Allegation #3 is therefore Sustained.

d. Allegation #4 that Officer-1 was absent from work without permission is

Officer-1 was scheduled to work a 9-hour shift on December 8, 2021, from 11:30 am until

V. RECOMMENDED DISCIPLINE FOR SUSTAINED ALLEGATIONS

a. Complimentary and Disciplinary History

Officer-1's complimentary history68 includes a total of 87 awards, including one
Superintendent's Award of Tactical Excellence and one Police Officer of the Month Award. His
disciplinary history69 includes a one-day suspension in July 2021 for a Preventable Accident.

b. Recommended Penalty

COPA has considered Officer-1's complimentary and disciplinary history. Officer-1
discharged his firearm at without justification, resulting in her death in violation
of Rules 1, 2, 3, 9 and 38. COPA also finds that Officer-1 failed to report that he discharged
a firearm, in violation of Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 22 and 39. Moreover, COPA finds that Officer-1
failed to secure his firearm, violating Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, and 13. COPA finds that Officer-1
was absent from work without permission, in violation of Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 27, 28 and 30.

Officer-1's actions were of the most grievous nature, a domestic related homicide. That
Officer-1 shot and ended her life is not only tragic, but flies in the face of
CPD's commitment to the sanctity of human life. Officer-1 failed in his duties to uphold the
laws, and instead committed the most egregious form of misconduct, ending the life of another
human being without justification. It is inherently obvious that this form of misconduct must not
be tolerated by the Department. It is for these reasons that COPA recommends a penalty of
Separation.
