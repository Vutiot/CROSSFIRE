A preponderance of evidence can be described as evidence indicating that it is more likely than
not that a proposition is proved.41 For example, if the evidence gathered in an investigation
establishes that it is more likely that the misconduct occurred, even if by a narrow margin, then
the preponderance of the evidence standard is met.

Clear and convincing evidence is a higher standard than a preponderance of the evidence but
lower than the "beyond-a-reasonable doubt" standard required to convict a person of a criminal
offense.42 Clear and Convincing can be defined as a "degree of proof, which, considering all the
evidence in the case, produces the firm and abiding belief that it is highly probable that the
proposition . . . is true."43

a. Allegation #1 that Officer-1 discharged a firearm at
without justification is Sustained.

COPA finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Officer-1 discharged his weapon
at Ms. without justification. There is no evidence in the record to suggest that the gunshot
wound to Ms. head was self-inflicted. Rather, the medical examiner ruled Ms.
death a homicide and Officer-1 has been charged with her murder.44 Although
Officer-1 has not been convicted of the charged offense and is presumed innocent until proven
guilty in a court of law, these administrative proceedings carry a lower burden of proof than
criminal proceedings.45 The charges pending against Officer-1 are relevant to COPA's
investigation and support the finding that Officer-1 engaged in misconduct in violation of
several Department rules.46 However, even absent the criminal charges against Officer-1,

COPA finds ample factual support in the record to conclude by a preponderance of the evidence
that Officer-1 discharged his firearm at Ms. without justification.

Specifically, the evidence shows that that Ms. died of a single gunshot wound to
the head and that Officer-1 was the last person to see her alive. In addition, Officer-1's left
hand tested positive for gunshot residue, indicating that he fired, or was at least in close proximity
to a discharged firearm. Importantly, the investigation uncovered that the fired cartridge casing
recovered from a shoe tray near Ms. body and the fired cartridge casing recovered from
Officer-1's bag found inside his residence had been fired from the same firearm. Although the
firearm has never been found, the evidence supports the conclusion that Officer-1 shot Ms.
with the firearm, which he then removed from her residence and hid or discarded.

Specifically, Officer-1 was observed on surveillance footage leaving Ms. apartment
with a firearm and he reported that he had left a Springfield Armory 9mm handgun on the top shelf
of Ms. bedroom closet. This handgun has never been found. COPA finds it more likely
than not that Officer-1 used the firearm he had at Ms. residence to shoot Ms.
and then disposed of the firearm.

There is also compelling evidence showing that Officer-1 removed Ms. cell
phone from her residence and possibly discarded it at an unspecified location near the 015th District
Station. Specifically, cell phone data indicated that the cell phones belonging to Officer-1 and
Ms. were together from 9:15 pm as they simultaneously travelled from Ms.
residence to an unspecified location near the 015th District Station, where Ms. cell
phone ceased transmitting to nearby cell towers at approximately 9:49 pm.

There is no evidence that anyone other than Officer-1 was involved. No one other than
Officer-1 entered or exited Ms. apartment between the time she was last seen alive
on surveillance cameras and when her body was discovered by the responding officers. Although
Officer-1 reported last seeing Ms. at 4:30 pm on December 8, 2021, security footage
from her apartment complex showed that Officer-1 did not leave Ms. apartment
until 9:06 pm that evening. Officer-1 was alone when he left her residence and Ms.
was not seen leaving her residence. Ms. was found shot dead the next day.

Furthermore, the evidence Officer-1 attempted to cover up his involvement in Ms.
death also supports a finding that he discharged his firearm at Ms. without
justification. Officer-1 initially reported that he last saw her at approximately 4:30 p.m. on
December 8 although video evidence showed him leaving at approximately 9:00 p.m. that day.47
He also reported that he had left a firearm in her residence, which was not found in her locked
apartment the day her death was discovered. It also appears that Officer-1 called Ms.
phone after he left her residence, but cell phone tower records showed that his own phone was in
the same location as Ms. at the time. Taken together, it is reasonable to infer that Officer-1
sought to mislead the police about key facts related to the incident.

b. Allegation #2 that Officer-1 failed to report that he discharged a firearm
is Sustained.

COPA finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Officer-1 failed to report that he
discharged a firearm in violation of Department rules. CPD rules 21 and require members to report
any information concerning a crime or unlawful action or other improper conduct. Rule 39 requires
members to immediately report a firearm discharge to a sergeant. There is no evidence that Officer-1
notified anyone at CPD to report his firearm discharge. Allegation #2 is therefore Sustained.

c. Allegation #3 that Officer-1 failed to secure a firearm registered to him is
Sustained.

Department policy requires sworn members "to secure ALL firearms which they own or
possess, in a manner that a reasonable person would believe will prohibit access by unapproved
individuals, theft, or loss."49 In addition, Department members are required to secure all firearms
in their possession or under their control in a manner that will prevent them from being accessed
or used by children.50 To protect children from accessing firearms, Department members are
required to secure their firearms by either a device or mechanism that would render the firearm
temporarily inoperable, place it in a secure locked box or container, or place it somewhere that a
reasonable person would believe it to be secure from a minor under the age of 14 years.51
Department policy also prohibits officers from lending their firearms to others.52

Here, the evidence shows that Officer-1 failed to secure two of his firearms. First,
Officer-1 admitted that he had left a firearm on the top shelf of Ms. bedroom closet,
which was never found. By his own admission, Officer-1 had lent a firearm to Ms. in
violation of Department policy. Second, Officer-1 kept a firearm at Ms. home for her
protection.53 Evidence technicians subsequently recovered a firearm54 in a bedroom closet inside
