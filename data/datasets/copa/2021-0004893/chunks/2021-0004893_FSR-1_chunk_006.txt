A preponderance of evidence can be described as evidence indicating that it is more likely than
not that a proposition is proved.49 If the evidence gathered in an investigation establishes that it is
more likely that the misconduct occurred, even if by a narrow margin, then the preponderance of
the evidence standard is met.
Clear and convincing evidence is a higher standard than a preponderance of the evidence but
lower than the "beyond-a-reasonable doubt" standard required to convict a person of a criminal
offense.5° Clear and Convincing can be defined as a "degree of proof, which, considering all the
evidence in the case, produces the firm and abiding belief that it is highly probable that the
proposition . . . is true."51
IV. ANALYSIS AND CONCLUSION
a. Allegation #1 that Officer Tyler discharged a firearm at
without justification is Sustained.
COPA finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Officer Tyler's use of deadly force
against violated Department policy. Based on the totality of the circumstance, COPA
finds that his weapons discharge was not objectively reasonable, necessary, or proportional. Here,
there is no evidence in the record to suggest that the gunshot wound to head was
self-inflicted, or that was armed or posed an imminent threat to Officer Tyler. Officer
Tyler did not call 911 or otherwise report an incident that would have justified his use of force.
Rather, the medical examiner ruled death a homicide and Officer Tyler has been
charged with her murder.52 Although Officer Tyler has not been convicted of the charged offense
and is presumed innocent until proven guilty in a court of law, these administrative proceedings
carry a lower burden of proof than criminal proceedings.53 The charges pending against Officer
Tyler are relevant to COPA's investigation and support the finding that Officer Tyler engaged in
misconduct contrary to Department policy.54 However, even absent the criminal charges against
Officer Tyler, COPA finds ample factual support in the record to conclude by a preponderance of
the evidence that Officer Tyler discharged his firearm at without justification.
