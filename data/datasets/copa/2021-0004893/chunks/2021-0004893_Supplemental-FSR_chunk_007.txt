b. Allegation #2 that Officer Tyler failed to report that he discharged a firearm
is Sustained.
COPA finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Officer Tyler failed to report that he
discharged a firearm in violation of Department rules. CPD rules 21 and require members to report
any information concerning a crime or unlawful action or other improper conduct. Rule 39 requires
members to immediately report a firearm discharge to a sergeant. There is no evidence that Officer
Tyler notified anyone at CPD to report his firearm discharge. Allegation #2 is therefore Sustained.
c. Allegation #3 that Officer Tyler failed to secure a firearm registered to him is
Sustained.
Department policy requires sworn members “to secure ALL firearms which they own or
possess, in a manner that a reasonable person would believe will prohibit access by unapproved
individuals, theft, or loss.”49 In addition, Department members are required to secure all firearms
in their possession or under their control in a manner that will prevent them from being accessed
or used by children.50 To protect children from accessing firearms, Department members are
required to secure their firearms by either a device or mechanism that would render the firearm
temporarily inoperable, place it in a secure locked box or container, or place it somewhere that a
reasonable person would believe it to be secure from a minor under the age of 14 years.51
Department policy also prohibits officers from lending their firearms to others.52
Here, the evidence shows that Officer Tyler failed to secure two of his firearms. First,
Officer Tyler admitted that he had left a firearm on the top shelf of Ms. bedroom closet,
which was never found. By his own admission, Officer Tyler had lent a firearm to Ms. in
violation of Department policy. Second, Officer Tyler kept a firearm at Ms. home for her
