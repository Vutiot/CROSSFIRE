d. Allegation #4 that Officer Tyler was absent from work without permission is
Sustained.
According to the Chicago Police Department's Attendance and Assignment Records,
Officer Tyler was scheduled to work a 9-hour shift on December 8, 2021, from 11:30 am until
8:30 pm.64 The record65 indicates that Officer Tyler reported for duty or was at least present at the
015th District at approximately 1:50 pm on December 8, 2021. However, by 4:30 pm, Officer
Tyler was seen entering residence and did not leave the residence until 9:06 pm,
more than four hours after arriving. There is no evidence that Officer Tyler requested time off or
adjusted his hours on December 8, 2021. Thus, the preponderance of the evidence shows that
Officer Tyler was absent from duty without proper authorization on December 8, 2021.66
Moreover, there is no evidence that Officer Tyler promptly reported his absence from duty as
required by Department policy.67 Allegation #4 is therefore Sustained.
V. RECOMMENDED DISCIPLINE FOR SUSTAINED ALLEGATIONS
a. Complimentary and Disciplinary History
Officer Tyler's complimentary history68 includes a total of 87 awards, including one
Superintendent's Award of Tactical Excellence and one Police Officer of the Month Award. His
disciplinary history69 includes a one-day suspension in July 2021 for a Preventable Accident.
b. Recommended Penalty
COPA has considered Officer Tylers complimentary and disciplinary history. Officer Tyler
discharged his firearm at without justification, resulting in her death in violation
of Rules 1, 2, 3, 9 and 38. COPA also finds that Officer Tyler failed to report that he discharged
a firearm, in violation of Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 22 and 39. Moreover, COPA finds that Officer Tyler
failed to secure his firearm, violating Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, and 13. COPA fmds that Officer Tyler
was absent from work without permission, in violation of Rules 2, 3, 5, 6, 27, 28 and 30.
Officer Tyler's actions were of the most grievous nature, a domestic related homicide. That
Officer Tyler shot and ended her life is not only tragic, but flies in the face of
CPD's commitment to the sanctity of human life. Officer Tyler failed in his duties to uphold the
laws, and instead committed the most egregious form of misconduct, ending the life of another
human being without justification. It is inherently obvious that this form of misconduct must not
be tolerated by the Department. It is for these reasons that COPA recommends a penalty of
Separation.
